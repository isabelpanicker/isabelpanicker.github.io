About

I am a Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering PhD candidate in the Jamali Lab at Georgia Tech working on developing liquid-phase transmission electron microscopy as a robust single-particle tracking tool to characterize the complex behaviors of soft matter systems at the nanoscale.

My work sits at the intersection of soft matter physics and nanotechnology, where direct visualization allows me to connect nanoscale dynamics to the emergent behavior of these systems.

02

Research

Liquid-phase TEM

Developing chip supports and imaging workflows to observe nanoparticle behavior in situ, including graphene-based supports and machine-learning segmentation of noisy micrographs.

Active Matter

Quantifying polarity-resolved active transport in Pt-PS Janus nanomotors through single-particle tracking, connecting propulsion mechanisms to measurable trajectory statistics.

Nanorheology

Using functionalized nanoparticle probes to characterize the local mechanics of coacervate and biomolecular condensate systems by passive microrheology.

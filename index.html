01 / About

About

I study how colloids and nanomaterials move, assemble, and organize in fluid environments. My work combines liquid-phase transmission electron microscopy with single-particle tracking to resolve dynamics that are difficult to capture by conventional imaging.

I am currently a PhD researcher at Georgia Tech, working across soft matter, complex fluids, and active nanomaterials. I am interested in bridging direct nanoscale observation with quantitative models of transport and self-organization.

02 / Research

Research

liquid-phase TEM

Imaging colloidal dynamics in liquid

Developing chip supports and imaging workflows to observe nanoparticle behavior in situ, including graphene-based supports and machine-learning segmentation of noisy micrographs.

active matter

Janus nanomotor transport

Quantifying polarity-resolved active transport in Pt-PS Janus nanomotors through single-particle tracking, connecting propulsion mechanisms to measurable trajectory statistics.

complex fluids

Coacervates and nanorheology

Using functionalized nanoparticle probes to characterize the local mechanics of coacervate and biomolecular condensate systems by passive microrheology.
